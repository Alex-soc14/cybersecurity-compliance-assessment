# Incident Response Policy

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish a formal process for identifying, reporting, containing, responding to and recovering from information security incidents, while minimizing their impact on the organization.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees and contractors, company information and personal data, information systems, applications, devices and services managed or used by the organization.

The policy also applies to relevant third-party services and suppliers where security incidents may affect the organization's information, systems or operations.

## 3. Incident Classification

Security incidents shall be classified according to their potential impact on information, systems, services and business operations.

### Low

Incidents with limited impact that can be managed through normal IT operations without significant disruption.

### Medium

Incidents that cause a significant but manageable impact on systems, information or business operations.

### High

Incidents that cause serious disruption, compromise sensitive information or significantly affect critical systems or business operations.

### Critical

Incidents that cause severe disruption, widespread compromise, major data loss or compromise of critical systems and require immediate management escalation.

## 4. Incident Reporting

All employees and contractors shall promptly report suspected or confirmed security incidents through the designated incident reporting process.

Reports should include, where available, a description of the incident, the affected systems or information, the time of detection and any relevant observations.

Employees shall not attempt to independently investigate or remediate serious security incidents unless authorized to do so.

The IT Department is responsible for receiving, assessing, classifying and coordinating the technical response to reported incidents.

Management shall be informed of High and Critical incidents and shall support escalation and decision-making when required.

## 5. Incident Response Process

The organization shall follow a structured incident response process consisting of the following phases:

### 5.1 Identification

The incident shall be identified, documented and initially assessed to determine its nature, scope and potential impact.

### 5.2 Containment

Appropriate measures shall be taken to contain the incident and prevent further damage or unauthorized access.

### 5.3 Eradication

The root cause of the incident shall be addressed and malicious or unauthorized activity shall be removed from affected systems.

### 5.4 Recovery

Affected systems and services shall be safely restored to normal operation. Recovery activities shall be documented and systems shall be monitored for signs of further compromise.

### 5.5 Post-Incident Review

Following significant incidents, the organization shall perform a post-incident review to identify the root cause, lessons learned and corrective actions required to reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

## 6. Incident Documentation

All security incidents shall be recorded in a centralized Incident Register.

The Incident Register shall contain, where applicable:

* Incident identification number
* Date and time of detection
* Date and time of occurrence, if known
* Person or system that detected the incident
* Description of the incident
* Affected systems, devices or information
* Incident classification and severity
* Initial assessment and potential impact
* Actions taken during containment
* Eradication and remediation activities
* Recovery activities
* Individuals or teams responsible for the response
* Relevant evidence and supporting documentation
* Communication and escalation activities
* Final resolution
* Root cause, where identified
* Lessons learned
* Corrective and preventive actions
* Date of closure

## 7. Evidence Preservation

Relevant evidence related to security incidents shall be identified, preserved and protected against unauthorized modification or deletion.

Evidence may include system logs, authentication records, emails, screenshots, files, network information and other relevant technical information.

Evidence shall be collected and handled in a manner that preserves its integrity and supports subsequent investigation.

Where required by law, regulation or organizational decision, relevant evidence may be provided to competent authorities or other authorized parties.

## 8. Testing and Review

The Incident Response Policy and related procedures shall be reviewed at least annually and following significant security incidents or major changes to the organization's systems, processes or threat environment.

The organization shall periodically test its incident response capabilities through appropriate exercises or simulations.

Test results shall be documented and used to identify weaknesses and implement corrective actions.
