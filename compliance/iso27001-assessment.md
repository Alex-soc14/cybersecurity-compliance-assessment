## ISO/IEC 27001 — Information Security Risk Management

### Assessment

The organization has established a risk register and uses a defined methodology to identify and assess information security risks.

However, the risk management process is not formally governed.

The risk assessment methodology has not been formally approved, risk acceptance criteria are not clearly defined, and responsibilities for individual risks are not consistently assigned.

The frequency for reviewing information security risks is also not formally established, and risk treatment decisions are not always documented.

**Status:** Non-Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. The information security risk assessment methodology is not formally approved.
2. Formal risk acceptance criteria have not been defined.
3. Risk owners are not consistently assigned.
4. The frequency and conditions for reviewing information security risks are not formally established.
5. Risk treatment and acceptance decisions are not consistently documented.

### Recommended Actions

1. Formally approve and document the information security risk assessment methodology.
2. Define risk acceptance criteria and approval responsibilities.
3. Assign a risk owner to each identified risk.
4. Establish periodic and event-driven risk reviews.
5. Document risk treatment, acceptance and residual risk decisions.

### Evidence

- Information Security Risk Management Procedure
- Approved risk assessment methodology
- Risk register with assigned risk owners
- Risk acceptance criteria
- Risk treatment records
- Periodic risk review records

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Information Security Policy

### Assessment

The organization has established several internal security rules covering areas such as password management, device usage, access control and incident management.

However, no formally approved Information Security Policy exists to define the organization's overall direction, objectives and commitments regarding information security.

Security responsibilities are not clearly defined, the security rules are not formally communicated to all employees, and no documented process exists for periodically reviewing and updating the policy.

**Status:** Partially Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. No formally approved Information Security Policy exists.
2. Information security roles and responsibilities are not clearly defined.
3. Security policies and rules are not formally communicated to all employees.
4. No documented process exists for reviewing and updating the Information Security Policy.

### Recommended Actions

1. Develop and formally approve an Information Security Policy.
2. Define information security roles, responsibilities and management commitments.
3. Communicate the policy to relevant employees and maintain evidence of acknowledgement.
4. Establish a formal process for periodically reviewing and updating the policy.

### Evidence

- Approved Information Security Policy
- Policy approval records
- Employee acknowledgement or training records
- Information security roles and responsibilities
- Policy review records

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Asset Management

The organization maintains an inventory of information and technology assets, including corporate laptops, cloud services, Microsoft 365, databases and internal applications.

However, the inventory is not fully complete and does not consistently identify the responsible owner for each asset.

Information is also not classified using a consistent classification scheme, and no formally defined review frequency exists for maintaining the asset inventory.

**Status:** Partially Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. The asset inventory does not include all relevant assets.
2. Asset ownership is not consistently assigned and documented.
3. No consistent information classification scheme has been implemented.
4. No formal process exists for periodically reviewing and updating the asset inventory.

### Recommended Actions

1. Complete the inventory of information and technology assets.
2. Assign an owner to each relevant asset.
3. Define and implement an information classification scheme based on sensitivity and business impact.
4. Establish a formal process for periodically reviewing and updating the asset inventory.

### Evidence

- Asset inventory
- Asset ownership records
- Information classification policy
- Asset review records
- Asset management procedure

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Access Control

### Assessment

The organization uses individual user accounts and assigns access rights primarily according to job roles.

Password policies are in place and multi-factor authentication is used for some accounts.

However, user permissions are not subject to formal periodic reviews, and some users have broader privileges than necessary.

The organization also lacks a documented process for managing access changes when employees change roles and for ensuring timely deactivation of accounts when employees leave the organization.

**Status:** Partially Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. No formal periodic review of user access rights and privileges.
2. Some users have privileges beyond those required for their roles.
3. No documented process exists for modifying access rights following role changes.
4. No formal control ensures timely deactivation of accounts when employees leave the organization.

### Recommended Actions

1. Establish periodic access and privilege reviews.
2. Apply the principle of least privilege.
3. Implement a documented joiner, mover and leaver process.
4. Ensure timely deactivation of accounts when employment ends.
5. Maintain evidence of access reviews and account deactivation.

### Evidence

- User access register
- Access review reports
- Privileged account register
- Joiner, mover and leaver procedure
- Offboarding records
- Account deactivation records

5. Perform documented post-incident reviews and lessons learned.
6. Periodically test and review the incident management process.

### Evidence

- Incident Management Procedure
- Incident register

- Incident response records
- Post-incident review records

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Business Continuity and Backup Management

### Assessment

The organization performs backups of critical data and stores them separately from the primary systems.

However, no formal Disaster Recovery Procedure has been established.

Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO) have not been defined for critical systems, and backup restoration tests are not performed according to a formally established schedule.

The organization also lacks a structured process for maintaining operations during major incidents and clearly assigning responsibilities during a crisis.

**Status:** Non-Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. No formal Disaster Recovery Procedure exists.
2. RTO and RPO have not been defined for critical systems.
3. Backup restoration tests are not performed according to a formally defined schedule.
4. No structured business continuity process exists for major incidents.
5. Crisis management roles and responsibilities are not clearly documented.

### Recommended Actions

1. Establish and formally approve a Disaster Recovery Procedure.
2. Define RTO and RPO for critical systems and services.
3. Establish periodic backup restoration tests and document their results.
4. Develop a Business Continuity Procedure for major disruptions.
5. Define crisis management roles and responsibilities.
6. Periodically review and test the continuity and recovery procedures.

### Evidence

- Disaster Recovery Procedure
- Business Continuity Procedure
- RTO/RPO documentation
- Backup restoration test records
- Disaster recovery test results
- Crisis management records

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Supplier and Third-Party Security

### Assessment

The organization relies on several external suppliers, including cloud, IT, SaaS and email service providers.

Supplier selection considers factors such as cost, functionality and service reliability.

However, cybersecurity requirements are not consistently integrated into the supplier selection and management process.

**Status:** Partially Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. No standardized security questionnaire is used to assess suppliers.
2. No formal cybersecurity risk assessment is performed before engaging critical suppliers.
3. Security requirements are not consistently included in supplier contracts.
4. Suppliers are not formally classified according to their criticality.
5. No periodic security review process exists for suppliers.

### Recommended Actions

1. Introduce a standardized supplier security questionnaire.
2. Perform risk assessments before engaging critical suppliers.
3. Classify suppliers according to their criticality and security risk.
4. Include appropriate cybersecurity requirements in supplier contracts.
5. Establish periodic security reviews for critical suppliers.

### Evidence

- Supplier Security Assessment Questionnaire
- Supplier risk assessments
- Supplier criticality register
- Supplier contracts and security clauses
- Periodic supplier security review records

2. No phishing simulation program has been established.

- Training attendance/completion records
- Phishing simulation reports

## ISO/IEC 27001 — Vulnerability Management

### Assessment

The organization performs security checks during some software releases and normally installs security updates.

However, no formal Vulnerability Management Procedure has been established.

Vulnerabilities are not managed through a centralized register, remediation deadlines based on severity have not been formally defined, and no formal escalation process exists for critical vulnerabilities.

Security testing of the web application is also performed only occasionally.

**Status:** Non-Compliant

### Identified Gaps

1. No formal Vulnerability Management Procedure exists.
2. No centralized vulnerability register is maintained.
3. No remediation deadlines are defined according to vulnerability severity.
4. No formal escalation process exists for critical vulnerabilities.
5. Web application security testing is not performed on a regular basis.

### Recommended Actions

1. Establish a formal Vulnerability Management Procedure.
2. Implement a centralized vulnerability register.
3. Define severity levels and remediation timeframes.
4. Establish an escalation process for critical vulnerabilities.
5. Perform periodic vulnerability scanning and security testing of critical systems and applications.
6. Document remediation activities and verify that vulnerabilities have been effectively resolved.

### Evidence

- Vulnerability Management Procedure
- Vulnerability register
- Vulnerability scan reports
- Penetration testing reports
- Remediation tickets
- Critical vulnerability escalation records
- Evidence of vulnerability remediation
